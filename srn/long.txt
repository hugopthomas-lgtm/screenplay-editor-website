INT. UNIVERSITY CORRIDOR - NIGHT

A corridor of closed doors. Strip lights buzz. One door is open, throwing a rectangle of light onto the floor.

NORA MBEKI (41), coat still on, walks the corridor reading from a phone. She stops at the open door.

INT. SEMINAR ROOM 306 - CONTINUOUS

A long table, twenty chairs, one occupied. TOBIAS FENN (63) sits at the far end with a stack of paper in front of him and a red pen he is not using.

NORA
They told me you'd gone home.

TOBIAS
They tell everyone that. It saves conversation.

He does not look up. Nora comes in, puts her bag on a chair, does not sit.

NORA
The building closes at ten.

TOBIAS
The building has been closing at ten for thirty-one years. I have never once been in it at ten.

NORA
It's twenty past.

Now he looks up.

TOBIAS
(mildly)
Then we are both criminals.

Nora sits. Between them, the stack of paper. She reads the top page upside down.

NORA
Is that the Kramer thesis?

TOBIAS
It is forty scripts by nineteen-year-olds, and they are all the same script.

NORA
They can't all be the same script.

TOBIAS
A young man returns to the village where his father died. It rains. There is a dog. The dog survives.

NORA
(beat)
That's a good film.

TOBIAS
It is forty good films.

He finally uncaps the red pen, looks at it, caps it again.

CUT TO:

INT. SEMINAR ROOM 306 - LATER

The stack has moved to the middle of the table. Two mugs now. The window is black.

NORA
Can I ask you something you won't like?

TOBIAS
You may ask. I reserve the right to enjoy it.

NORA
Why do you still mark them on paper?

A long pause. Tobias turns a page over, smooths it with his palm.

TOBIAS
Because on paper I can see how long they held a shot.

NORA
You can see that on a screen.

TOBIAS
I can see the number on a screen. On paper I can feel the weight of it in my hand. Two pages of silence is heavy. Two pages of talking is light. The screen makes them the same.

NORA
That's not true and you know it.

TOBIAS
(cheerfully)
It is entirely not true. I am sixty-three and I like paper.

Nora laughs, once, and it surprises her.

INT. SEMINAR ROOM 306 - LATER

Nora has taken off her coat. She is reading a script from the middle of the stack. Tobias watches her read.

NORA
This one's different.

TOBIAS
Page?

NORA
Page four. She doesn't answer the phone.

TOBIAS
Ah. Yes. That one.

NORA
The whole scene is her not answering the phone.

TOBIAS
Two and a half pages of it.

NORA
(reading)
"She lets it ring. She lets it ring. She lets it ring."

TOBIAS
Three times. He wrote it three times. I nearly crossed out two of them.

NORA
Why didn't you?

TOBIAS
Because the third one is the one that hurts.

Nora puts the script down. Something in the room has changed.

NORA
Who is he?

TOBIAS
First year. Sits at the back. Has never once spoken.

NORA
Does he know it's good?

TOBIAS
Nobody has told him.

DISSOLVE TO:

EXT. CAMPUS - NIGHT

The two of them cross an empty car park. Sodium lights. A fox stops, considers them, moves on.

NORA
I have a meeting tomorrow about the software.

TOBIAS
The software.

NORA
The licences. Forty thousand a year and it doesn't run on the machines the students actually own.

TOBIAS
So buy different machines.

NORA
With what?

TOBIAS
(not unkindly)
This is the part of the conversation where I am useless.

NORA
You're not useless. You're just on paper.

INT. NORA'S CAR - NIGHT

Rain begins. Nora does not start the engine. Tobias sits in the passenger seat with his seatbelt already on, patient as a schoolboy.

NORA
Can I tell you what I actually want?

TOBIAS
Please.

NORA
I want to see the writing happen. Not the final file. The mess. The version where she answers the phone, and the version where she doesn't, and the three minutes between them.

TOBIAS
That is not a piece of software. That is a person paying attention.

NORA
It could be both.

Tobias considers this. The rain gets heavier.

TOBIAS
When I was your age I marked a script by a girl who wrote the same scene eleven times. Eleven. I gave her a two-one and told her to make up her mind.

NORA
And?

TOBIAS
She stopped writing.

Silence. The wipers are off. The windscreen blurs completely.

TOBIAS (CONT'D)
So no. I do not think it is only a person paying attention. I think the person gets tired, and the person is wrong, and the person is me.

CUT TO:

EXT. HEADINGTON HILL - NIGHT

Nora's car climbs the hill, tail lights in the rain.

INT. NORA'S CAR - CONTINUOUS

TOBIAS
Left at the lights. There is no need to take me to the door.

NORA
I'm taking you to the door.

TOBIAS
There is no need.

NORA
There is rain.

He accepts this.

EXT. TOBIAS'S HOUSE - NIGHT

A narrow terrace. The car stops. Neither of them moves.

TOBIAS
The boy at the back.

NORA
Yes.

TOBIAS
Tell him it's good. Not from me. From you. It counts for more from someone he does not have to believe.

NORA
He'll ask what was good about it.

TOBIAS
Then tell him: the third time she lets it ring.

He gets out. Shuts the door. Leans down to the window, which she lowers.

TOBIAS (CONT'D)
Nora.

NORA
Yes.

TOBIAS
Buy the machines the students own.

He goes in. The light in the front room comes on, then off again.

INT. SEMINAR ROOM 306 - MORNING

Sun through dirty glass. The table is empty except for one script, squared up, a yellow sticky note on the cover.

Insert: the note, in a hard old-fashioned hand: "PAGE 4. DO IT AGAIN."

A DOOR opens. A STUDENT (19), hood up, comes in for a chair and stops when he sees the script with his name on it.

He reads the note. He reads it again.

He sits down, in the wrong chair, at the wrong end of the table, and starts to write.

FADE OUT.

THE END
