If a woman lets a telephone ring three times on the page, how many times does it ring in the film?

NORA
Three.

TOBIAS
Wrong. It rings for as long as the editor can bear it, which is never three.

NORA
Then why write it three times?

TOBIAS
Because the writer has to feel it three times before anybody else feels it once.

Nora looks at her spreadsheet. She closes the laptop.

NORA
Tobias. Why are you calling me?

A pause on the line. The rain on his window is audible.

TOBIAS
Because I am going to stop in July, and I would like somebody to know what I know before I do.

INT. NORA'S KITCHEN - CONTINUOUS

Nora does not move.

NORA
Does the department know?

TOBIAS (V.O.)
The department will know in April, in an email, from somebody in an office who has never read a script.

NORA
That's a terrible way to end thirty-one years.

TOBIAS (V.O.)
It is the standard way. I have seen it done to better people.

NORA
Then let's do it differently.

TOBIAS (V.O.)
(amused)
With what? A ceremony?

NORA
With a class.

EXT. CAMPUS - DAY

Spring, or the idea of spring. Buds on the one tree. Students on the grass with coats still on, because it is England.

INT. SEMINAR ROOM 306 - DAY

The table has been pushed to the wall. Twenty chairs in a circle. ADAM sits with his script in his lap. Around him, STUDENTS, and NORA at the back, and TOBIAS in the middle with nothing in his hands.

TOBIAS
Today somebody reads out loud and everybody else keeps their opinion in their mouth until the end.

He looks at Adam. Adam does not move.

TOBIAS (CONT'D)
Mr Cheung. Page four.

ADAM
Out loud?

TOBIAS
That is what the page is for.

Adam stands, because he does not know you can read sitting down. His hands are not steady. He begins.

ADAM
(reading)
"Interior. Flat. Night. She is at the sink with her back to the room. The telephone starts to ring."

He looks up. The room is looking at him. He looks back down.

ADAM (CONT'D)
(reading)
"She lets it ring."

A beat.

ADAM (CONT'D)
(reading)
"She lets it ring."

Another. Longer.

ADAM (CONT'D)
(reading)
"She lets it ring."

Silence in the circle. Real silence, the kind you can hear a chair make.

A STUDENT
(quietly)
Is she going to answer it?

ADAM
No.

The student nods slowly, and something happens in the room that nobody will be able to describe in the bar afterwards.

TOBIAS
Thank you. Sit down before you enjoy it.

Adam sits. Nora, at the back, is not looking at Adam. She is looking at Tobias, and what she sees is a man watching a thing he made outlive him.

INT. CORRIDOR OUTSIDE 306 - LATER

Students file out. Adam is stopped by two others who want to say something and do not know how. He says it for them.

ADAM
It's not finished.

STUDENT TWO
It's better than finished.

They go. Adam stands alone in the corridor with the script against his chest.

INT. SEMINAR ROOM 306 - CONTINUOUS

Nora stacks chairs. Tobias does not help, which is his way of staying.

NORA
I have my number.

TOBIAS
For February.

NORA
For February. It is smaller than forty-one thousand and it does something else.

TOBIAS
Which is?

NORA
It buys the thing they already have. Whatever machine, whatever house. We stop asking them to move.

Tobias considers the stacked chairs.

TOBIAS
That is either very clever or the beginning of a long complaint from a man in an office.

NORA
Both.

TOBIAS
(pleased)
Both is correct.

EXT. OXFORD STREET - NIGHT

The wet street, shop lights, the last bus. Nora walks Tobias to the stop, slower than either of them needs to walk.

TOBIAS
You will have to teach it.

NORA
The software?

TOBIAS
The format. The software is a plumber. Somebody still has to know why the water goes where it goes.

NORA
I know why.

TOBIAS
Then say it to me now, so that I have heard you say it.

Nora stops walking. This matters more than she expected.

NORA
Because the page is a promise about time. A minute of a stranger's life for every page, and if you lie about it, they find out in the dark, in a room, with their coat on their knees.

Tobias looks at her for a moment too long.

TOBIAS
Thirty-one years and nobody has put it that well.

NORA
You put it better in 1996. I read the article.

TOBIAS
(getting on the bus)
Then plagiarise me properly. Put my name on it.

The doors close. The bus pulls away up the hill. Nora stands in the rain longer than she needs to.

INT. SEMINAR ROOM 306 - NIGHT

Empty. The chairs are stacked. On the table, one thing: a red pen, capped, left deliberately in the middle where somebody will find it.

INT. ADAM'S ROOM - NIGHT

A small room, a desk made of a door, the old laptop open. On the screen, a document, and in it a scene that is finally in the right shape.

Adam reads it. He changes one word. He reads it again.

He types, at the bottom of the page, in capitals, the only part of a script anybody gets to write for themselves.

Insert: on the screen, the words "THE END".

He looks at it. He deletes it.

ADAM
(to himself)
Not yet.

He scrolls back to the top and starts again at page one, which is what writers do, and which is the whole of this story.

EXT. HEADINGTON HILL - DAWN

The hill in first light. Rain gone. The road wet and shining. A fox crosses, unhurried, and is gone into the hedge.

The first bus of the day climbs the hill with its lights still on.

FADE OUT.

THE END
