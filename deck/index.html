01
The problem

A script that is not formatted correctly does not get read.

So every screenwriter buys and learns a second application for one job: putting the words in the right columns.

Final Draft sells at $249, plus around $80 per upgrade. 93% of professional screenwriters have used it, and it is used on 95% of film and television productions. It is thirty years old and it is not where anyone writes their first draft.

The first draft happens in Google Docs, in Word, on a phone at 2am. Then the writer copies it into the formatting software, which is the part nobody wants to do.

02
The product

Type the way you already type. The page formats itself.

Screenplay Editor turns any text editor into a screenwriting application. It formats as you type, handles scene headings, character cues, dialogue and transitions, produces a production breakdown, and exports to Final Draft and PDF.

06
The market

A screenwriter is a professional, and a screenwriting tool is a professional tool.

The screen and script writing software market is worth about $236 million in 2026, growing 14.4% a year. It is not a hobby category.

Selling formatting software to a screenwriter is the same transaction as selling accounting software to an accountant. The work cannot be delivered without it. That is why a thirty-year-old desktop application can charge $249 and still own the category.

The risk, in my own words

Almost all my customers arrive through Google's two stores.

I would rather tell you that myself than have you find it in the data. It is also the thing the entire product strategy exists to end, and that work is already shipping.

The position I am building toward is Grammarly for screenplay formatting: one formatting engine that follows the writer into whatever they already write in, rather than a Google Docs feature. Google Docs is simply the surface with the most screenwriters on it today.

Why this is affordable for one person

The formatting engine is never forked. Each new surface is a thin adapter over the same engine and the same test suite, so a fix costs one fix and not one per build. Adding a surface is a week, not a rewrite. That is the mistake I watched other multi-platform tools make.
