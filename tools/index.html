Free tools for screenwriters.

Seven of them run entirely in your browser: your script stays on your computer and nothing is uploaded. The last two need a model to draw and to write, so they send a short summary and say exactly what it holds. None of them asks you to create an account. I built them because I write my own films in Google Docs and kept wishing they existed.

Converter

Convert your screenplay to anything

Drop in a PDF, a Final Draft file or a Fountain file, and take it out as a Google Doc that is already formatted, a .fdx, or a .fountain.

 Checker

Check your format before you send it

The notes a reader would write on your script: type size, margins, headings, long speeches, names spelled two ways.

 Breakdown

Turn your script into a breakdown sheet

Every scene, interior or exterior, day or night, its location and its cast, in a coloured spreadsheet.

 Structure

Lay out your beats and start writing

The fifteen beats at their real page numbers for the length you are writing, in a document already divided.

 Title page

Make a title page that looks right

Four fields, and the title page a reader expects, centred and spaced the way the industry sets it.

 Running time

How long does your script run?

A real estimate with an honest range, and how much of the film is people talking.

 Characters

See who carries your script

Who speaks, how much, in how many scenes, and the page where they walk out of the film.

 Loglines

Get three loglines from your script

It reads your pages first, then writes three loglines on three different angles. Not a form you fill in.

 Poster

Make a poster for your film

Drop in your screenplay and get the poster that film would have. The one thing here you will want to show somebody.

One conversion at a time

The same converter, with the answer already picked. Each page says what survives the trip and what stays behind, because that part is different every time.

Fountain to Final Draft

Your .fountain, typed into a .fdx

Fountain hides the type of a line in its punctuation. Final Draft writes it down. This reads the first and writes the second.

 Final Draft to Fountain

Your .fdx as plain text

Plain text you can still read in twenty years, in any editor, under version control if you want it there.

 PDF to Final Draft

A PDF you can write in again

A PDF records ink, not meaning. The converter measures the columns your script uses and reads the format back out of them.

 PDF to Fountain

A PDF back into plain Fountain

Same reading of the columns, written out as text, with every line that could be misread marked so it comes back right.

 Final Draft to Google Docs

Your .fdx in a Doc with the right margins

Nothing is guessed, because a .fdx already records what each paragraph is. The margins are the industry ones, to the point.

 Fountain to Google Docs

Your .fountain in a Doc with the right margins

Forced elements, title page, notes and boneyard all read. The asterisks stay out of your document.
