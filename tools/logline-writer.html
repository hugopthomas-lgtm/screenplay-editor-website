The other tools on this site never send anything anywhere. This one has to, because writing needs a model. It gets a summary, never your pages.

Why three, and why these three

A logline has to carry a want, a pressure and a cost, and most drafts carry one of the three well and the other two badly. So you get one of each, built on the same film, and the useful moment is the one where you notice which angle makes your story sound like a story.

None of them is your logline. They are three readings of what you wrote, and the closest one is a starting sentence to argue with.

Questions about loglines

I write my own films in Google Docs, so I built the tools I kept wishing existed.

Ask me something
What makes a good logline?
One sentence naming who the story is about, what they want, what stands in the way and what it costs. It gives the premise away rather than teasing it, and it never opens with in a world where.
Does my whole script get sent somewhere?
No. Your browser reads the file and builds a short summary: the main character names, the locations, your page count, and about thirty lines of dialogue taken from the beginning, the middle and the end. That summary is what gets sent, and the page shows you exactly what it holds before you press the button.
Why does it need my script at all?
Because a form can only hand back what you typed into it. Reading the pages is the difference between a logline about your film and a logline about the genre you picked from a menu.
How many can I make?
A few a day from the same network, which is enough to try each angle on a couple of drafts. The limit is there because writing costs money on our side.
